# Voucha for Community Creators: Trust and Moderation Built In from Day One

Building a community is easy. Building one where the quality holds up over time — where good contributions rise, noise gets filtered, and members trust what they're reading — is hard. Most generic forum tools give you a discussion space and leave the rest to you.

Voucha communities come with trust and moderation built into the infrastructure, not bolted on afterward.

## What Communities Are

A community is a named, moderatable space within Voucha's topic graph. Members post, discuss, and vote. Posts published inside a community can also appear in the broader public feed, giving your community content organic reach beyond its membership.

Communities are distinct from topics. A topic is a canonical data entity — a credit card, a GPU model, an AI tool. A community is an ongoing conversation space, often tied to multiple topics, but shaped by the moderators rather than the taxonomy.

## Getting Started

Any signed-in account with a username can create communities. Paid memberships do not gate ownership; they add higher contribution limits, more voting weight, and more AI agent prompt slots for moderators who want native automation support.

| Plan | Community Ownership Cap | Community Creation Rate Limit |
| ---- | ----------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Free | Unlimited               | 2/day                         |
| Plus | Unlimited               | 5/day                         |
| Pro  | Unlimited               | 10/day                        |

You can reach the community creation page at `/communities/create`.

## Roles and Moderation

Every community has three role levels:

- **Owner** — full control, including settings, member management, and role assignment
- **Moderator** — can approve posts, manage members, and configure the publication queue
- **Member** — can post and vote within the community

As owner, you control who becomes a moderator. Moderators who are paid members can also allocate AI agent prompt slots within the community (Plus members get 3 slots total across all their communities, Pro members get 10).

## How Posts Get Published

Communities can operate in two modes:

**Open**: All posts by members are immediately published to the community feed and, if the post is set to broadcast everyone, to the public feed as well.

**Moderated**: Posts require moderator approval before appearing in community feeds. This gives moderator teams editorial control over what represents the community publicly.

This setting is configurable per community in the community settings panel.

## Who Can Join

You control how people join your community:

- **Public join** — any Voucha user can join directly
- **Application flow** — prospective members answer custom questions you define; you approve or reject applications
- **Invite codes** — share a link that grants direct membership, bypassing the application queue

Application questions let you qualify members. For a credit card community, you might ask about churning experience. For a hardware community, you might ask about primary use case. This context helps moderators make better decisions about who to let in.

## What Happens If Your Membership Lapses

If your membership lapses, your communities stay with you. Paid-only benefits such as extra AI agent prompt slots stop applying until the membership is active again, but community ownership does not transfer solely because a plan expired or paused.

Communities can still be archived through moderation, owner action, or policy enforcement. An archived community is read-only: existing members can still read all content, public communities remain visible to non-members, but new posts and new memberships are blocked. No data is deleted.

## Why Build a Community Here

Generic forum tools give you a space for people to talk. Voucha communities give you a space where the talking generates structured, trust-weighted intelligence that compounds over time.

When a member posts a review inside your community, their trust score weighs their vote. When they submit a data point, it feeds into the broader community-verified aggregate. When the community votes on posts, those votes carry weight in the wider ranking algorithms.

Your members' contributions surface to people who follow the associated topics — not just to your community members. Voucha treats community content as a layer on top of the public knowledge graph, not as a silo.

You're not just moderating a forum. You're curating a community where the quality of information compounds over time, backed by the same trust infrastructure that runs the rest of the platform.
