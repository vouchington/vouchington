# Trust Is the Infrastructure: How Community Trust Works on Voucha

On Voucha, trust isn't a feature you turn on — it's the underlying system that determines what you see, how content is ranked, which data points count in aggregates, and whose referral links surface first. Everything flows through it.

The more you participate, the better the platform works for you — and understanding why makes that participation more deliberate.

## Trust Is on Everything

Most platforms apply trust signals narrowly. Reddit has karma. Amazon has verified purchases. Yelp has elite reviewers. Each tracks a single dimension.

Voucha applies trust across every surface:

- **Posts** receive upvotes and downvotes
- **Reviews** receive votes weighted by the voter's own trust score
- **Data points** carry contributor trust levels
- **Domains** (news sources) accumulate community trust scores
- **Users** build trust across every vertical they contribute to
- **Feeds** (RSS sources) are trust-scored by the community

This breadth is deliberate. When trust exists everywhere, gaming any single surface doesn't help you. An account that submits fake data points but doesn't vote, review, or participate will have a trust profile that reflects that imbalance.

## The Social Graph

The social graph is what makes Voucha's trust system personal rather than generic.

When you follow someone, you're making a trust declaration. That declaration shapes your experience across the platform:

- **Feed ranking** — Content from people you follow appears higher in your feeds
- **Referral link priority** — When you view a topic with referral links, links from people in your social graph rank above links from strangers
- **Review weighting** — Reviews from people you follow are more prominent on product pages
- **Data point visibility** — Data points from your network are highlighted in aggregate views

Two users looking at the same credit card page might see different referral links ranked first, different reviews featured, and different data points highlighted — all based on their respective social graphs. The platform is personalized not by algorithm, but by your actual circle of trust.

The practical effect: the platform gets better as your network grows. Following people whose judgment you trust is one of the highest-leverage actions you can take on Voucha.

## How Trust Compounds

Trust on Voucha builds across interactions, verticals, and time. Here's how each dimension works.

### Contribution History

A user who submits 50 accurate credit card data points over six months builds significant trust in the credit card vertical. Their future data points carry more weight in aggregate calculations. Their reviews rank higher. Their referral links get a trust boost.

This isn't a simple point counter. It's contextual. The same user might have minimal trust in the hardware vertical if they've never contributed there. Trust is earned per domain and transfers partially — a user trusted in one vertical starts with a small advantage in others, but needs to earn vertical-specific trust through actual contributions.

### Vote Alignment

As the trust system matures, vote weight calibration comes into play. Users whose votes consistently align with moderator assessments and community consensus earn higher vote weight. Users whose votes consistently diverge — or who show patterns consistent with manipulation — see their vote weight diminished.

This creates a self-reinforcing quality signal. Good-faith participants naturally accumulate influence. Bad-faith actors naturally lose it.

### Time Decay

Trust signals decay over time. A data point submitted last week is more relevant than one from two years ago. A review from an active contributor carries more weight than one from an account that's been dormant for 18 months. This decay keeps aggregate data current rather than historical.

The decay algorithm uses a 30-day half-life on vote scores — a contribution's influence is halved every 30 days. Active content and active contributors maintain their influence; stale content naturally fades.

## How Voucha Compares to Other Trust Systems

### Reddit Karma

Reddit karma is a single global number that aggregates upvotes across all subreddits and content types. A user with 100,000 karma from posting memes has the same display as a user with 100,000 karma from writing detailed product analyses. As a trust signal for actual decisions, it's not very useful.

Voucha's trust is contextual (per-vertical), compositional (builds across interaction types), and functional (directly affects content ranking and data weighting). It's not a vanity metric — it's what the platform runs on.

### Hacker News Karma

HN's system is closer to useful — karma from upvoted submissions and comments, with downvoting gated behind a karma threshold. But it's still a single number serving a single community. Voucha extends the same basic idea across multiple verticals with vertical-specific trust accumulation.

### Amazon Verified Purchase

Amazon's "verified purchase" badge is binary — either you bought through Amazon or you didn't. It tells you nothing about the reviewer's history, judgment quality, or relationship to you. A verified purchase from a first-time reviewer and one from someone who's written 500 reviews look identical.

Voucha's contributor tiers (unverified, verified, trusted) are graduated and based on contribution history, not just purchase verification. And the social graph layer means you can prioritize reviews from people you personally trust over reviews from strangers, regardless of badge level.

## Defense Against Manipulation

Every trust system faces attack. The question is how resilient it is and how expensive attacks become.

### Why Astroturfing Is Hard on Voucha

Astroturfing — fake accounts to artificially promote or demote products — is a persistent problem on review platforms. Voucha makes it structurally difficult for several reasons:

**Contribution gating.** New free accounts have a 7-day waiting period before posting and writing reviews. They can vote once verified, but early votes carry minimal weight until the account ages in. Paid accounts get immediate posting and review access, but payment is itself a trust signal (and creates a paper trail).

**Trust-weighted aggregates.** Even after the gating period, new accounts start with minimal trust weight. Their data points and votes have near-zero impact on aggregates and rankings. Building trust requires sustained, consistent, high-quality contributions over time — exactly what astroturfers can't do efficiently at scale.

**Social graph filtering.** Even if fake accounts submit data points, those points are unlikely to be connected to real users. The social graph naturally isolates unconnected accounts, suppressing their visibility for most people.

**Behavioral signals.** Submission velocity, data point consistency, session patterns, and referral link click patterns feed into automated detection. Coordinated campaigns create detectable patterns that authentic users don't.

**Community reporting.** Users can flag suspicious accounts and contributions. Community reports feed into trust calculations and moderator review queues.

### The Cost Calculation

On Amazon, creating a fake review requires one purchase and one account. On Reddit, one account and one post. On Voucha, meaningfully influencing aggregates requires multiple aged accounts with sustained contribution histories across multiple interaction types, social connections to real users, and consistent behavioral patterns — all while evading automated detection.

The cost-per-impact of manipulation on Voucha is designed to be high enough that it's not worth the effort for most actors.

## What This Means for You

The trust system works best when you participate actively and authentically:

- **Vote regularly.** Your votes improve content ranking for everyone and calibrate the trust system.
- **Submit data points.** Every structured data point you contribute makes the aggregates more accurate and builds your trust profile.
- **Follow thoughtfully.** Your social graph is your personal trust filter. Curate it deliberately.
- **Write reviews when you have real experience.** Substantive reviews with structured sub-ratings contribute more to your trust profile than quick takes.

The platform is designed so that the things that help you — tracking your own data, finding trusted recommendations, building a network of reliable sources — are the same things that help everyone else. That alignment is deliberate. It's what makes the system hold.
