# Why Voucha: Real Recommendations from People You Trust

When you're about to apply for a credit card, buy a GPU, or switch to a new AI tool, you ask yourself one question: who do I trust, and what did they actually experience?

It sounds simple. It isn't. The places you'd naturally go to answer it — review sites, forums, search engines — each have a structural problem that gets in the way. Voucha exists because none of them have fixed it.

## What's Broken

### Review sites serve advertisers, not you

NerdWallet, The Points Guy, Wirecutter — these sites produce polished content, but they get paid when you click and apply. A credit card paying $200 per approved application gets more editorial attention than one paying $50, regardless of which card is actually better for you.

That doesn't mean every recommendation is wrong. It means you can never be sure whether a pick reflects genuine analysis or financial incentive. That uncertainty is the problem.

### Reddit has the data, but you can't use it

Reddit is the closest the internet has to honest consumer opinions. Real people share real experiences in r/churning, r/buildapc, r/homelab. The problem is that the data is trapped in comments.

Someone in a thread from eight months ago reported being approved for the Chase Sapphire Preferred with a 740 credit score and 3 recent inquiries. Someone else last week was denied with a 760 score but 6 inquiries. Both are valuable. Neither is discoverable. And there's no way to see them side by side, let alone add them up with hundreds of similar reports to find the actual pattern.

### Amazon reviews are gamed

Amazon's review system was a good idea that has been systematically broken. Incentivized reviews, manipulation services, and vine programs have inflated the average product rating to 4.3 out of 5 stars. Everything looks roughly the same. There's no way to know whether a reviewer used the product for a week or two years, and no context to help you decide whether their experience resembles yours.

### Forums are scattered

Specialized communities — credit card boards, hardware enthusiast forums, AI tool discussion groups — contain real expertise, but they're spread across dozens of sites with different formats and no way to cross-reference. The knowledge is there. Getting to it requires manual work.

## How Voucha Works

Voucha brings together three things that haven't existed on the same platform before.

### Real numbers from real people

Every contribution on Voucha includes specific, queryable fields — not just free text. When someone reports a credit card application outcome, they submit their credit score range, income bracket, number of recent inquiries, and result. When someone reports on hardware ownership, they log purchase date, failure status, and real-world performance.

That structure is what makes the numbers useful. Instead of reading through 50 comments and tallying approvals yourself, you can see "73% approval rate for applicants with 750+ credit scores" built from actual submissions. The more people contribute, the sharper the picture gets.

### Trust that actually means something

Voucha's trust system reflects how people actually decide who to believe. Votes on posts, reviews, data points, and sources create layered signals that shape what you see and how it's weighted.

This is fundamentally different from Reddit's karma, where upvotes on a meme count the same as upvotes on a careful product analysis. On Voucha, trust is built by category and by track record. A user who consistently submits accurate credit card data has earned credibility in that domain. Their contributions carry more weight in credit card results than a brand-new account's.

### Your friends' opinions, first

When you follow someone on Voucha, their contributions surface higher in your feed, their referral links rank above strangers', and their reviews appear more prominently. This is what no review site or forum offers: the ability to filter the platform through the people you actually know.

Your friend's experience with a credit card means more to you than a stranger's. Voucha reflects that in every ranking.

## What This Looks Like

You're researching a specific travel credit card.

On NerdWallet, you find a glowing review — but the card pays the highest affiliate commission in its category. On Reddit, you find scattered data points across three different threads from different months. On the card issuer's site, you find a marketing page.

On Voucha, you see the aggregate approval rate broken down by credit score range, built from hundreds of submissions. You see that a friend you follow was approved last month with a similar profile. You see comparison reviews where real cardholders rate this card against alternatives on rewards value, annual fee, customer service, and perks. And if you decide to apply, the referral link that surfaces first belongs to someone in your social graph — not a stranger.

That's the difference between navigating trust manually and having trust built into the infrastructure.

## Beyond Credit Cards

The same foundation that powers credit card results extends to any category where real-world experience matters. Hardware owners log real-world failure rates and longevity. AI tool users track adoption patterns and what makes them switch. Car buyers share owner-reported reliability.

Voucha launched with credit cards. Hardware, AI tools, cars, and more are on the same foundation.

## What We're After

Every decision you make should be backed by people you trust — not anonymous reviews from accounts you can't verify, not editorial content shaped by affiliate revenue, not marketing from whoever is selling to you.

Real recommendations, from real people, weighted by trust.

That's Voucha.
