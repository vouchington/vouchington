# Voucha for Lifelong Learners

Good lectures are not hard to find. Finding them without wading through ten thousand reaction videos is the hard part. A conference talk from 2012 can still be the best explanation of its subject. A feed built on what is new will never show it to you.

This is how to build a learning list on Voucha from people you trust.

## Follow the sources

Many of the best teaching channels publish an RSS feed.

- **Lecture series.** University courses on YouTube.
- **Conference talks.** Recordings from engineering, science and design conferences.
- **Math and science channels.** Long explainers from independent teachers.
- **Documentaries.** Filmmakers and broadcasters that post full films.
- **Podcasts.** Interview shows and audio courses.

Add each as a source. YouTube channels use an address of the form `https://www.youtube.com/feeds/videos.xml?channel_id=` plus the channel's ID. Voucha lists the videos and plays them in the page. Podcasts keep your place in each episode. You can import or export your list as OPML, so it moves with you. See [RSS Is Back](./rss-feeds-comeback.md).

## Let communities do the first pass

A [community](/communities) is a group built around a subject, such as astronomy, woodworking or machine learning. Its owners and moderators can keep [community-curated lists](/communities?has_list_items=true) of sources, topics, posts and domains. A good list is a reading list someone already filtered.

Join the communities for your subjects. Open their lists. Follow the sources you like and skip the rest.

## Choose teachers, not algorithms

When you follow a person, you can see what they post and what they think is worth your time. If they recommend a bad one, mute them. If they recommend a good one, keep following. Over a few weeks you learn whose judgment matches yours. That is the point of your circle of trust.

Votes on Voucha are weighted by who cast them, and one-day-old accounts count for very little. So a talk that long-standing members vote up is worth a look. See [How Trust Works on Voucha](./trust-signals-explained.md).

## Keep the list tidy

Mute channels that slide into noise. Mute topics that pull you off course. The aim is a short list you will actually use.

## Where to start

1. Pick one subject you want to learn this year.
2. Join the community for it, and read its lists.
3. Add two or three channels or shows to your sources.
4. Come back in a month and mute whatever you skipped.

More on the idea behind this: [Personalized Feeds Narrow You](./personalized-vs-curated.md) and [Mentioned Is Not Recommended](./mentioned-vs-recommended.md).
