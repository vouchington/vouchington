# How to Curate YouTube for an Aging Parent

In the comments under HackerTube, two people described the same worry. One had an 87-year-old father who watched negative war documentaries for hours. A geriatrician had told him to stop, and he could not. Another had a father-in-law, 81, stuck in a loop of low-effort World War II videos, many of them AI-generated.

Both asked for the same tool: something that would filter by keyword. It is a fair ask. No tool today does the whole job. Here is what works now, in order of effort.

## 1. Fix YouTube first

These steps cost nothing and change what the recommendations do.

- **Turn off watch history.** In the account's data and privacy settings, pause YouTube history. With no history, the home page has far less to feed on.
- **Use the Subscriptions page.** It lists new uploads from channels your parent chose, in order, with nothing added. Make it the bookmarked start page instead of the home page.
- **Use "Don't recommend channel."** Tap the three dots on any video from a channel that pulls them down. Do it every time one shows up.
- **Clear the existing history.** Old watching shapes today's feed.

Sit with them for the first session. The point is not to take away something they enjoy. It is to change what shows up next to it.

## 2. Pick the channels together

Make a list of 10 to 20 channels that fit their interests and leave them calmer. Ask them. Local history, trains, birdwatching, gardening, old comedy, long-form interviews, concerts and church services all work.

Check each channel yourself before you add it. Look at a few recent uploads. If the titles use capital letters and the word "SHOCKING," skip it.

## 3. Follow those channels on Voucha

Every YouTube channel has an RSS feed, and Voucha reads RSS. Add each channel to your account as a source, and its new videos appear in one list with no recommendations around them.

1. Find the channel's feed address. It looks like `https://www.youtube.com/feeds/videos.xml?channel_id=` followed by the channel's ID.
2. Open your channels page and use Add source.
3. Paste the address.

Voucha treats these feeds as video. You can watch inside the page. You can also import and export your list of channels, so you can hand a list to a sibling or keep a backup.

A caveat: Voucha follows the channels you add. It does not replace the player on your parent's tablet. For a parent who is comfortable with a browser, a bookmark to your Voucha page may be enough. For one who is not, you can read the list to them or print titles. Be honest about that.

## 4. Mute what slips through

When a feed or a topic keeps bringing up something you do not want, mute it. You can mute topics, users, feeds, domains and communities. Muted videos stop showing up in your lists. There is no keyword mute yet, so mute by source.

## 5. Let someone else do the curating

You do not have to find every channel yourself. Join a [community](/communities) around the subject your parent likes. Its moderators keep [community-curated lists](/communities?has_list_items=true) of sources, so you can follow a list someone else vetted. You can also follow the people whose picks you trust and see what they add.

## The honest limit

None of this fixes loneliness or boredom, which are often why the videos get watched in the first place. A call, a visit, or a weekly video watched together does more than any filter.
