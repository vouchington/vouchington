# Copyright and the DMCA on Voucha

Voucha respects copyright. If someone posts your work without permission, you can ask us to take it down. If your post is taken down by mistake, you can ask us to put it back. This page explains how that works. [How Copyright Complaints Work](./copyright-complaints.md) walks through each step and the timeline.

## Where Things Stand

Our copyright process handles US claims under the Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA). It opens once our designated agent is registered with the US Copyright Office. Until then, we don't accept copyright notices. The designated agent status page, `/copyright/designated-agent`, shows the current status and will list the agent's contact details once they're published.

## What the Process Covers

Right now the process covers images in Voucha posts. A notice names each image by the post's link. For other problems, such as spam or harassment, report the post instead.

## A Person Reviews Every Notice

We may use automated tools to screen or summarize a notice, and automatic provisional withholding may hide an image pending review. A person reviews the decision. Each notice explains whether automated tools assisted or imposed a provisional restriction. We don't act on a notice that's missing required information.

A case records a claim. It doesn't mean the poster infringed anything, and it doesn't prove that the person who filed it owns the work.

## What Happens When We Accept a Notice

- **The image is hidden in that post, for everyone.** It's hidden in every country, not just the US. The rest of the post stays up.
- **Other posts aren't affected.** If the same image appears in another post, that post isn't changed unless the notice names it too.
- **Nothing is deleted.** We keep the image so we can restore it if the decision changes.
- **Both sides are told.** The poster gets an email and an in-app notice with the reasons and redress routes. The notifier gets a decision email. Accepted cases appear at `/copyright/notices`, where each signed-in participant sees their own notices.

## What Other Members Can See

Signed-in members can see accepted cases: the dates, the post link, whether the image is hidden, and the claimant's current public Voucha profile. Case pages never show anyone's legal name, email, mailing address, signature, or the evidence behind a case.

## If Your Image Was Taken Down

You have two options, and you can choose either one:

- **Appeal.** Ask a moderator to take another look, for example because you own the work or have a license. An appeal is an ordinary review. It doesn't start a legal clock.
- **Counter-notice.** A legal statement, under penalty of perjury, that the image was removed by mistake or misidentification. We send your name, address, phone number and signature to the person who filed the notice. If your counter-notice is valid, we restore the image 10 to 14 business days after we receive it. The exception is when the claimant tells our designated agent, before then, that they've filed a federal lawsuit or a Copyright Claims Board (CCB) claim against you about the same image. In that case the image stays hidden while that case is open.

[How Copyright Complaints Work](./copyright-complaints.md) explains both options in detail.

## Repeat Infringers

Our repeat-infringer policy is at `/copyright/repeat-infringer-policy`.

## False Claims

Under 17 U.S.C. § 512(f), anyone who knowingly makes a material misrepresentation that material is infringing, or that material was removed or disabled by mistake or misidentification, can be liable for damages, including costs and attorneys' fees. This applies to notices and counter-notices alike. Before you file, make sure your claim is accurate, and consider whether the use could be fair use. If you're unsure, talk to a lawyer.
