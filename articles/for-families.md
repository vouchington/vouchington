# Voucha for Families: Make Household Decisions Together

Big household decisions are rarely made alone. You're figuring out which credit cards actually cover your spending, whether the mesh router everyone recommends actually holds up with four people on it, or what appliance makes sense to buy. And you're doing all of this in coordination with your partner — two adults, sometimes different priorities, always shared consequences.

Voucha is built around trust relationships. Follow the people you trust. Use their data. Get to a decision faster, together.

## Your Household Card Portfolio

Most households end up with credit cards they accumulated by accident — a store card here, a bank offer there. A deliberate strategy can be worth thousands of dollars a year.

The starting point is mapping what your household actually spends:

| Category        | Typical Monthly Spend | Best Card Type            |
| --------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Groceries       | $800-1,200            | 4-6% grocery bonus card   |
| Gas             | $200-400              | 3-5% gas/transit card     |
| Dining          | $300-600              | 3-4% dining card          |
| Online shopping | $500-1,000            | 5% rotating category card |
| Travel          | Varies                | Points-based travel card  |
| Everything else | $1,000+               | 2% flat cash-back card    |

Voucha's credit card data points and comparison reviews help you find the specific cards with the best rates in each category, with real approval odds so you know your chances before you apply.

## The Authorized User Move

Adding a partner as an authorized user is one of the most underused household optimization tools:

- **Combined spending toward SUBs** — Two people spending toward a minimum spend requirement doubles your capacity
- **Credit building** — Authorized users benefit from the primary cardholder's payment history
- **Shared rewards** — Both users earn on the same account
- **Referral bonuses** — One partner refers the other for cards with referral programs

Voucha's data points track authorized user outcomes — whether authorized user history reported correctly to credit bureaus, whether spending from authorized users counted toward sign-up bonuses — so you can see how this strategy has played out for other households before you try it.

## A Portfolio That Covers Every Category

A household's combined spending is significant, and the best card for each category depends on current bonus structures and your specific pattern.

A three-card household setup might look like:

- **Card A**: 4% on groceries and streaming (both adults carry this)
- **Card B**: 3% on dining and travel (primary for restaurants and trips)
- **Card C**: 2% on everything else (the default)

Data points from other households with similar spending help you validate whether this combination is actually optimal or whether a different setup performs better for your situation.

## Shared Product Research

Big household purchases — appliances, electronics, furniture, vehicles — benefit from the same community data that powers individual decisions on Voucha.

When the household needs a new dishwasher, aggregated reliability data from people who've owned it for two years matters more than a showroom impression. When you're choosing a family vehicle, owner-reported data on real-world fuel costs and repair history helps more than a dealer's pitch.

Both partners can follow the same topics, share data points, and access the same community intelligence — so you're looking at the same information when you sit down to decide.

## Family Hardware Decisions

Households have specific technology needs that differ from individual users:

- **Networking** — Mesh WiFi systems, parental controls, device management for a home with 15+ connected devices
- **Shared computing** — Family computers, shared subscriptions, storage solutions
- **Smart home** — Which ecosystem (HomeKit, Google, Alexa) actually works for your household's mix of devices
- **Durability** — Hardware that survives children, pets, and daily family use

Data points from other households — "This mesh system reliably covers 2,400 sq ft with four people streaming simultaneously" — are more useful than professional reviews conducted in empty test environments.

## How to Get Started as a Household

1. **Both partners create accounts** — Each adult gets their own profile and trust history
2. **Follow each other** — Share your social graph so both of you see the same prioritized content
3. **Audit your current cards** — List what you have, what each earns, and find the gaps in category coverage
4. **Check data points** — Look up approval odds for cards that would fill your portfolio gaps
5. **Submit your own data** — Your card application outcomes, ownership experiences, and reviews help other families in similar situations
6. **Create a landing page** — If either partner shares recommendations with friends, a landing page organizes those referral links in one place

Your household is a data source too. The cards you carry, the appliances you've owned, the tech decisions you've made — all of it helps families like yours avoid the same mistakes and find what actually works.
