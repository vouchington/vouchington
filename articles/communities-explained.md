# Voucha Communities: How They Work

Communities on Voucha are user-created spaces organized around specific topics — a credit card niche, a hardware category, a shared interest. They're built on top of Voucha's trust system, so everything that happens inside a community is connected to the rest of the platform.

Here's what communities are, how to find them, and what to expect as a member.

## What Makes Them Different

Most discussion platforms treat communities as isolated containers. A post in a subreddit stays in that subreddit. Activity inside a Discord stays inside that Discord.

On Voucha, communities are connected. Posts can surface in the broader public feed. Contributors build a trust track record that applies platform-wide. Data points submitted inside a community feed into the same aggregate database that everyone on Voucha queries.

Your activity inside a community doesn't stay siloed — it compounds.

The quality bar is also self-enforcing. The same trust-weighted voting that applies everywhere on Voucha applies inside communities. Low-quality posts from low-trust accounts carry less weight. High-trust contributors surface higher.

## Finding Communities

Browse communities at `/communities`. Each community card shows the name and description, how many members it has, and when it was last active.

You can also encounter communities through topics. If you're on a topic page for a specific credit card and a community is actively discussing it, that community surfaces in the topic's feed.

## Joining

Depending on how the community owner has configured it, there are three ways to become a member:

**Direct join.** Click "Join" on the community page. You're in immediately.

**Application.** The community requires you to answer questions before joining. The owner or moderators review your answers and approve or reject the application. Questions vary — some communities ask about experience level, some about specific interests, some just ask why you want to join.

**Invite link.** Someone shares a direct invite URL with you. Clicking it grants immediate membership without going through an application.

## What Happens Inside

Once you're a member, you can post, comment, and vote. Depending on the community's settings, your posts may appear immediately in the community feed, or they may go to a moderator approval queue first.

Posts in a community can also be set to broadcast to the broader public feed. A discussion you start in a credit card community might surface to people who follow credit card topics, even if they're not community members.

## Trust Carries Over

Everything you do inside a community feeds your platform-wide trust profile:

- Votes you cast carry your trust weight
- Reviews and data points you contribute go into the global aggregate
- Your reputation in one community transfers to your reputation everywhere

There's no separate community reputation system. Being a trusted contributor in a hardware community means your hardware reviews carry weight platform-wide, not just locally.

## Settings You'll Notice

**Open vs. moderated.** Open communities publish posts immediately. Moderated communities hold posts for review. If your post doesn't appear right away, it's in the approval queue.

**Application questions.** If a community requires an application, you'll answer questions before being granted membership. These are set by the owner and reflect what the community values.

**Invite-only.** Some communities don't appear in public search and are only accessible via invite link. These tend to be more specialized or curated.

## If a Community Gets Archived

Occasionally a community gets archived because of owner action, moderation, or policy enforcement. An archived community is read-only: you can still see everything, but new posts and new memberships are blocked.

Archived communities aren't deleted. If the issue that caused the archive is resolved, the community can be unarchived and resume normal operation. A paid membership is not required solely to own an active community.
