# The Chase 5/24 Rule: Everything You Need to Know

If there's one rule that changes how serious rewards earners plan their entire card strategy, it's Chase's 5/24 rule. Ignore it and you'll waste applications on denials that were entirely predictable. Understand it and you can plan around it for years, not just months.

## What Is the 5/24 Rule?

The 5/24 rule is Chase's unofficial policy of automatically denying credit card applications from anyone who has opened five or more new credit card accounts — across all issuers, not just Chase — in the past 24 months.

Chase has never publicly confirmed this rule by name. It surfaced around 2015, when people with strong credit scores started getting denied for no apparent reason other than the number of recently opened accounts. Thousands of reported outcomes later, the pattern is unmistakable.

The rule is simple in concept but has enough nuances to fill this entire article.

## How to Count Your 5/24 Status

Your 5/24 count includes:

- **All personal credit cards** opened in the last 24 months, regardless of issuer
- **Authorized user accounts** added in the last 24 months (these appear on your credit report as new accounts)
- **Store cards and co-branded cards** that report to credit bureaus as credit card accounts
- **Cards opened through product changes** in some cases (if the product change resulted in a new account number)

Your 5/24 count does **not** include:

- **Business credit cards** from most issuers (Capital One and Discover are notable exceptions that report business cards to personal credit reports)
- **Charge cards** from American Express (historically these didn't count, though recent data points have made this murkier)
- **Closed accounts** that are still within the 24-month window (closing a card does not remove it from your count)

### Counting Example

Suppose over the past 24 months you've opened:

| Card                         | Type     | Counts toward 5/24? |
| ---------------------------- | -------- | ------------------- |
| Chase Sapphire Preferred     | Personal | Yes                 |
| Amex Gold                    | Personal | Yes                 |
| Chase Ink Business Preferred | Business | No                  |
| Citi Double Cash             | Personal | Yes                 |
| Capital One Venture X        | Personal | Yes                 |
| Amex Blue Business Plus      | Business | No                  |

In this scenario, you're at 4/24. You have room for one more personal card before hitting the limit.

## Which Chase Cards Are Subject to 5/24?

Most Chase cards fall under the 5/24 rule, but not all. The rule generally applies to:

**Subject to 5/24:**

- Chase Sapphire Preferred and Reserve
- Chase Freedom, Freedom Flex, Freedom Unlimited, Freedom Rise
- Chase Ink Business cards (Preferred, Cash, Unlimited)
- Most Chase co-branded cards (Southwest, United, IHG, Hyatt, Marriott, British Airways, Aer Lingus)
- Amazon Prime Visa

**Historically exempt from 5/24** (based on reported data points, subject to change):

Some co-branded cards have periodically bypassed 5/24, particularly through targeted or in-branch offers. These exceptions are inconsistent and shift over time. What people are actually experiencing right now is the only reliable signal.

## The Authorized User Loophole

Authorized user accounts count toward your 5/24 number, but Chase has historically allowed applicants to call the reconsideration line and explain that one or more of their accounts are authorized user accounts rather than primary accounts. In many reported cases, the representative removes those from the count and approves the application.

This isn't guaranteed. Reports suggest a success rate of roughly 60 to 80 percent when calling reconsideration for AU-related denials, though the outcome depends heavily on the representative and your overall profile.

If someone added you as an authorized user without your knowledge — a parent building your credit, for example — you can also dispute the account with the credit bureaus to have it removed from your report entirely.

## Business Cards: The Strategic Bypass

Since most business credit cards don't report to personal credit bureaus, they don't increment your 5/24 counter. This creates a powerful strategy:

1. Open Chase personal cards while under 5/24
2. Fill gaps between personal applications with business cards from Chase and other issuers
3. Earn bonuses on business cards without affecting your 5/24 count
4. Stretch your time under 5/24 by months or even years

A common progression looks like this:

- Month 1: Chase Sapphire Preferred (1/24)
- Month 4: Chase Ink Business Preferred (still 1/24)
- Month 7: Chase Freedom Flex (2/24)
- Month 10: Amex Blue Business Plus (still 2/24)
- Month 13: Chase United Explorer (3/24)

By alternating personal and business cards, this hypothetical churner stays under 5/24 for over a year while still earning six sign-up bonuses.

You don't need a traditional business to apply for business cards. Selling items online, freelance work, tutoring, or even a genuine plan to start a business can qualify. Misrepresenting your business would be fraud, but the definition of "business" is broader than many people realize.

## Strategies for Those Already Over 5/24

If you're already at or above 5/24, you have several options:

**Wait it out.** Cards drop off your 5/24 count exactly 24 months after they were opened (based on the date reported to credit bureaus, which may differ by a few days from your actual approval date). If you opened a card on March 15, 2024, it typically stops counting around March 2026.

**Target non-5/24 cards.** While waiting for your count to drop, apply for cards from issuers without similar restrictions: Amex, Capital One, Citi, Barclays, US Bank. Build out your rewards portfolio in other ecosystems.

**Watch for pre-approvals.** Chase occasionally extends pre-approved offers that bypass the 5/24 rule. These can appear when checking your offers while logged into Chase's website or when visiting a Chase branch. Reports of pre-approval bypasses are among the most closely tracked in the community because they represent rare opportunities.

**Modified double dip.** Historically, applicants could apply for two Chase personal cards on the same day, with only the first counting toward 5/24 at the time of the second application. Chase has largely closed this loophole, but variations are occasionally reported. Don't count on it, but keep an eye on what others are seeing.

## How People Track What's Actually Happening

The 5/24 rule is not static. Chase adjusts its enforcement, exceptions, and edge cases over time, and these changes are rarely announced. The only way to know what's actually happening right now is from people who have applied recently.

For example, the community has caught:

- **Shifting exemptions.** Cards that were once exempt from 5/24 have been pulled under the rule, and vice versa. People reported these changes within days of them taking effect.
- **Velocity sensitivity.** Some applicants at 3/24 or 4/24 have been denied, suggesting Chase sometimes applies stricter thresholds for people who've opened several cards in quick succession — even if they're technically under 5/24.
- **Regional and profile variations.** Approval patterns sometimes differ based on existing Chase relationship, banking history, or geographic region. Only large-scale reporting can surface these patterns.
- **Reconsideration success rates.** Whether calling the reconsideration line actually works — and under what circumstances — only becomes clear when many people report what happened.

## Planning Your Chase Strategy

The optimal approach to 5/24 depends on your goals:

**Maximize Chase bonuses:** Stay under 5/24 as long as possible by supplementing with business cards. Prioritize the highest-value Chase SUBs first (Sapphire Reserve/Preferred, then Ink Business Preferred, then co-branded cards aligned with your travel patterns).

**Balanced approach:** Get two to three Chase personal cards, then branch out to Amex and other issuers. Accept that you'll go over 5/24 eventually and plan for a cooling-off period to get back under.

**Travel-focused:** If your travel patterns align with a specific airline or hotel — say, United or Hyatt — prioritize the corresponding Chase co-branded cards while under 5/24, then shift to Amex for Membership Rewards flexibility.

Whatever your approach, the 5/24 rule rewards planning. Every personal card application should be intentional, because each one moves you one step closer to the threshold. Check your current count, look at what people with your profile are experiencing, and make each application count.
